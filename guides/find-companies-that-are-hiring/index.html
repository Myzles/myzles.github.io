How to find companies that are hiring

Job ads are one of the best public signals of what a company is about to spend money on. A company hiring three SDRs is building a sales team. One hiring its first data engineer is about to buy data tools. Here's how to find who's hiring, straight from the source, without paying for a job data feed.

1. Start with the careers page

Try /careers, /jobs and /join-us on the company's domain, or look for "Careers" or "We're hiring" in the footer. Big job sites like LinkedIn and Indeed copy listings from here, often days late, and keep closed roles up for weeks. The careers page is the up-to-date version.

2. Find the job board behind it

Most companies don't build their own careers page. They embed an applicant tracking system (ATS). Click any job and look at the address bar, or search the page source for these domains:

ATSPublic board address
Greenhouseboards.greenhouse.io/company or job-boards.greenhouse.io/company
Leverjobs.lever.co/company
Ashbyjobs.ashbyhq.com/company
Workableapply.workable.com/company
SmartRecruitersjobs.smartrecruiters.com/company
BambooHRcompany.bamboohr.com/careers

The company part (the slug) is usually the company name in lowercase, but not always. Copy it from a real job link rather than guessing.

3. Read the public job feed

Three of the big ones publish every open job as JSON you can open in a browser tab. No login needed:

https://boards-api.greenhouse.io/v1/boards/slug/jobs
https://api.lever.co/v0/postings/slug?mode=json
https://api.ashbyhq.com/posting-api/job-board/slug

Each job has a title, location, department and usually a posted or updated date. Paste the feed into any JSON-to-CSV converter and you have a spreadsheet of every open role.

4. Find companies hiring for a specific role

To go the other way, from a role to companies, search the job board domains directly:

site:boards.greenhouse.io "account executive" "remote"
site:jobs.lever.co "data engineer"
site:jobs.ashbyhq.com "head of marketing"

Every result is a live (or recently live) posting at a company using that ATS. LinkedIn's job search with the "Past week" date filter is the other quick way to see who's posting now.

5. Track it over time

One snapshot tells you who's hiring. Changes tell you who's growing. Keep a sheet with one row per company and columns for the date, number of open roles, and roles by team. Check weekly. What to look for:

  • New teams: a first marketing or security hire means a new budget.
  • Leadership roles: a new VP usually brings new tools within months.
  • Volume jumps: five new engineering roles in a week usually follows funding.
  • Tools in the job text: "experience with Salesforce" or "we use Snowflake" tells you their stack.

FAQ

How can I tell if a company is hiring?

Check its careers page, or the job board it uses (Greenhouse, Lever, Ashby and others). Those are more current than LinkedIn or Indeed, which copy listings and keep closed ones up.

Is there a free API for job postings?

Greenhouse, Lever and Ashby all publish public job board feeds as JSON for each company that uses them. You need the company's board slug.

Why is hiring data useful for sales?

New roles show where a company is about to spend: new teams, new leaders and specific tools named in job ads all point to upcoming purchases.
