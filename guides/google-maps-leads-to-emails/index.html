How to get emails for a Google Maps lead list

Google Maps is one of the best sources of local business leads: every plumber, dentist and gym in a city, with a phone number, a website and reviews. What it never gives you is an email, because a Google Business Profile has no email field. Here's how to get from a Maps search to a list with emails, by hand or with a script.

1. Export the Maps results

Copying listings by hand works for 20 places. For more, use a Google Maps scraper (there are several on Apify, plus browser extensions and paid lead tools). Whichever you use, make sure the export includes:

  • Place name and category
  • Website: this is the column the rest of this guide depends on
  • Phone, address and the Maps link, so you can keep them next to the email later

Search tip: Maps caps how many results one search shows, so split a big city into neighbourhoods or postcodes and run several searches rather than one.

2. Clean the website column

  • Drop empty rows into a separate tab. Places with no website are phone-only leads.
  • Strip tracking. Maps websites often carry ?utm_source=gmb or similar. Cut everything after the domain.
  • Dedupe on domain. Chains and franchises list the same website on every location, so 40 branches can collapse into one domain to check.
  • Flag non-websites. Some listings point to a Facebook page, Linktree or booking site instead of a real website. Those need a different approach.

The same Sheets formula from the list-of-websites guide works here, with the website in A2:

=LOWER(REGEXEXTRACT(REGEXREPLACE(A2,"^https?://(www\.)?",""),"^[^/?#]+"))

3. Find the email on each website

From here it's the same method as any list of websites. For each domain, check the homepage footer, then /contact, /about, /team, /imprint and /privacy. Search each page's source for mailto: and email patterns, drop junk such as image names and placeholders, and decode written-out addresses like info [at] acme [dot] com.

Local businesses have two quirks worth knowing:

  • Free mailboxes are normal. Lots of tradespeople use a Gmail or Outlook address as their real business email. Don't filter those out by default.
  • Wix and similar builders are common for local firms, and their emails often only appear after JavaScript runs. A plain HTTP fetch sees nothing; a real browser does.

The full walk-through, with a Google Sheets function and a Python script, is in How to find emails from a list of websites.

4. Join the emails back to the Maps rows

Keep the Maps data. A row with the business name, category, rating, address, phone and email is far more useful than a bare email. In Sheets, if your Maps tab has the domain in column B and your results tab has domain in A and email in B:

=IFERROR(VLOOKUP(B2, Results!A:B, 2, FALSE), "")

Because you deduped chains on domain, every branch picks up the same head-office email, which is usually what you want.

5. Check the domains take mail

nslookup -type=mx acme.com

No MX records means mail will bounce, so remove those rows before importing into a cold email tool. MX records don't prove a mailbox exists; treat this as a filter, not verification.

FAQ

Does Google Maps show email addresses?

No. A Google Business Profile has a phone number and a website, but no email field, so every Maps scraper returns phones and websites at most. The email has to come from the business's own website.

How do I get emails from Google Maps scraper results?

Take the website column from the export, remove empty and duplicate rows, then check each site's homepage and contact, about, team and imprint pages for an email. Join the results back to the Maps rows on domain.

What about places with no website?

Roughly, the smaller the business the more likely it only has a Maps listing or a Facebook page. You can call the Maps phone number or message the Facebook page, but there is no website to read an email from.

Is it legal to email businesses found on Google Maps?

Emailing a business's published address is common, but the rules depend on where they are: CAN-SPAM in the US, and GDPR and PECR in the EU and UK, which are stricter about emailing individuals.
