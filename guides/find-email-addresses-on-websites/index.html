How to find email addresses on a website

Most companies publish at least one email address. It's just rarely on the homepage. Here's where to look, how to read the addresses sites try to hide from bots, and what to do when you have a list of 500 websites instead of five.

1. Check the pages where emails usually live

Try these, in this order. Add them straight to the domain if there's no menu link:

  • /contact, /contact-us: the obvious one. Often just a form, but scroll to the bottom.
  • The footer of any page.
  • /about, /team, /our-people: small firms list each person with their own address.
  • /impressum or /imprint: German and Austrian businesses are legally required to publish one, and it almost always has an email.
  • /privacy: privacy policies usually name a contact for data requests, often a real inbox.
  • /careers, /press: separate jobs@ and press@ addresses.

2. Search the page source

Some addresses sit behind a "Email us" button or icon and never show up as text.

  1. Press Ctrl+U (or right-click, View page source).
  2. Press Ctrl+F and search for mailto:. Every hit is a clickable email link.
  3. Search for @ and the domain name too. Ignore addresses like name@2x.png (image files) and ones on tracking or font domains.

If the page is built with JavaScript, View source can come back almost empty. Open DevTools (F12), go to the Elements tab and search there instead: it shows the page after scripts have run.

3. Decode hidden and obfuscated addresses

  • Written out: sales [at] example [dot] com or sales(at)example.com. Swap the words back for @ and ..
  • Cloudflare email protection: the page shows [email protected]. Clicking it in a normal browser reveals the address. In the source it's a data-cfemail value: the first two hex characters are a key, and each following pair XOR'd with that key gives one character.
  • HTML entities: &#115;&#97;... looks like noise in the source but renders as a normal address on the page, so read it on the page.
  • Images: some sites show the address as a picture. You'll have to type it out.

4. When there's no email anywhere

About a third of small business sites only have a contact form. Your options:

  • Use the form. It's what they want you to do.
  • Check the company's LinkedIn, Facebook or Google Business profile, which sometimes list an email the site doesn't.
  • Guess the common patterns (info@, hello@, firstname@), knowing that a guess isn't an address.

Before you guess, check the domain can receive email at all:

nslookup -type=mx example.com

If it lists mail servers, the domain takes email. That doesn't tell you whether a particular mailbox exists, and many servers accept everything and bounce later. WHOIS records rarely help any more: since GDPR most of them are redacted.

Whatever you find, follow the rules where you send. In the US, CAN-SPAM requires a real address and an opt-out in commercial email. In the EU and UK, cold emailing individuals has stricter rules than emailing a generic company inbox.

5. Doing it for a list of websites

Steps 1 to 3 take two or three minutes a site. For 20 sites that's an hour. For 500, use a tool that visits the contact pages for you. Watch for ones that charge per page crawled (a site with ten pages costs ten times as much), and ones that return junk like example@domain.com or image filenames.

FAQ

How do I find a hidden email address on a website?

View the page source with Ctrl+U and search for mailto: and @. If the page shows [email protected], it's Cloudflare's protection: click it in a normal browser to reveal the address.

How do I check if an email domain is real?

Run nslookup -type=mx on the domain. If it lists mail servers, the domain accepts email. That doesn't prove a specific mailbox exists.

Is it legal to collect emails from websites?

Collecting a business's published contact address is common, but how you use it is regulated: CAN-SPAM in the US, and GDPR and PECR in the EU and UK, which are stricter about emailing individuals.

Why can't I find an email on a company website?

Many small businesses only publish a contact form. Check the imprint, privacy page and their social profiles, or use the form.
