How to find a small business owner’s email instead of info@

If you sell to local businesses (dentists, law firms, agencies, trades), you've probably hit the same wall as a lot of cold emailers: the Google Maps list gives you websites, the websites give you info@ or contact@, and those inboxes are read by a receptionist, if anyone. B2B databases rarely help, because they cover small local firms poorly.

The good news is that many small businesses do publish the owner's name, and often their email, on their own website. You just have to look in the right places.

When there's a name but no email

  1. Find the company's email format. Look for any personal address on the site, in a press release or in a PDF. If you see jane.smith@firm.com, the owner is probably first.last@ too.
  2. Guess the common patterns if you can't find one: first@, first.last@, firstlast@, f.last@. Small firms often use first@.
  3. Verify before you send. Run guessed addresses through an email verifier. Many small-business domains accept all mail (catch-all), so a "valid" result there is only a maybe. Keep guessed and listed emails labelled separately in your sheet.
  4. Use info@ with the owner's name. If nothing works, an email to info@ that starts "Hi Sarah" and is clearly for the owner gets forwarded far more often than a generic one.

Doing it for a whole list

By hand this is two to five minutes per business, which is fine for 20 and painful for 500. The usual workflow is:

  1. Export the businesses from Google Maps (or any list) with their websites.
  2. Pull every public contact from each site: emails, phones, contact form, socials. See getting emails for a Google Maps lead list.
  3. For each site, read the team, about and imprint pages for named owners and partners.
  4. Prefer a listed personal email, then a guessed one you've verified, then info@ addressed to the owner by name.

FAQ

How do I find the owner of a small business\’s email?

Check the team, about, imprint and bio pages on the business\’s own website. If you find a name but no email, work out the company\’s email format from any other personal address and verify the guess.

Why are my cold emails to info@ addresses not getting replies?

Generic inboxes are often read by a receptionist or not at all. Addressing the owner by name, or reaching a personal address, gets far more replies.

Is it legal to email business owners whose address is on their website?

Rules vary by country. In many places B2B cold email is allowed with an opt-out, while some EU countries are stricter. Check the rules where your recipients are.

Do B2B databases have small business owners?

Coverage of small local businesses is usually patchy. The business\’s own website and the Google Maps listing are often more current.

What is a catch-all email domain?

A domain that accepts mail to any address, so a verifier cannot confirm whether a specific mailbox exists. Treat guessed addresses on catch-all domains as unconfirmed.
