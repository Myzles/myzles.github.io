How to find HubSpot duplicate contacts with different email addresses

HubSpot treats the email address as the contact's identity. That means bob.miller@gmail.com from a webinar and robert.miller@acme.com from a sales call become two contacts, and no import or form will ever join them. These are the duplicates that hurt most: two owners, two sets of emails, and a split history.

Here's how to find them by hand with a spreadsheet, using the clues that survive a change of email: phone number, name (including nicknames) and company.

Step 1. Export the right columns

Go to Contacts, pick All contacts, click Export, and include these properties: Record ID, First name, Last name, Email, Phone number, Mobile phone number, Company name, City. Open the file in Google Sheets or Excel. Keep an untouched copy: merges in HubSpot can't be undone.

Step 2. Make a phone key

Phone numbers are the best clue, because people change emails far more often than mobile numbers. The trouble is formatting: (555) 010-2234, 555.010.2234 and +1 555 010 2234 are the same number.

In Google Sheets, with the phone in column E, add a column with the last 10 digits:

=RIGHT(REGEXREPLACE(E2&"", "[^0-9]", ""), 10)

In Excel 365:

=RIGHT(CONCAT(IFERROR(MID(E2,SEQUENCE(LEN(E2)),1)*1,"")),10)

Do the same for mobile phone. Ten digits suits US and Canadian numbers; for other countries, keep the last 9 or the full number with the country code.

Step 3. Make a name key with nicknames

Bob, Rob, Robbie and Robert are one person on a business card. Make a two-column lookup tab called Nicknames with the common ones:

NicknameName
bob, rob, robbie, bobbyrobert
bill, will, billywilliam
liz, beth, lizzie, betsyelizabeth
mike, mikeymichael
jim, jimmyjames
kate, katie, kathykatherine
davedavid
chrischristopher

Put one nickname per row. Then build the key (first name in B, last name in C):

=LOWER(TRIM(IFERROR(VLOOKUP(LOWER(TRIM(B2)), Nicknames!A:B, 2, FALSE), B2))) & " " & LOWER(TRIM(C2))

Add a company key too: lowercase the company name and strip "inc", "llc", "ltd" and "the", so "The Acme Co." and "acme co" line up:

=TRIM(REGEXREPLACE(LOWER(G2), "\b(the|inc|llc|ltd|co|corp|gmbh)\b|[.,]", ""))

(That one is Google Sheets; in Excel, do it with Find and Replace on a copy of the column.)

Step 4. Find the matches

Add count columns and sort:

Phone matches:          =IF(H2="", 0, COUNTIF(H:H, H2))
Name + company matches: =COUNTIFS(J:J, J2, K:K, K2)

Filter to rows where either count is 2 or more and sort by the key. Matching rows now sit next to each other. Read them as:

  • Same phone and same name key: almost certainly one person. Merge.
  • Same name key and same company, different phone or none: likely one person. Check the emails: a personal address plus a work address at that company is a strong sign.
  • Same phone, different names: often a shared office or household line. Don't merge on phone alone.
  • Same name only: leave it, unless something else connects them. Two John Smiths at different companies are two people.

Step 5. Merge in HubSpot

  1. Open the record you want to keep, usually the one with the work email and the most activity.
  2. Click Actions › Merge, search for the other record by its Record ID or email, and confirm.
  3. HubSpot keeps the primary email and adds the other address as a secondary email, so both still point to this contact.

On the Free plan this is one pair at a time. For more on that, see merging duplicates on HubSpot Free.

FAQ

Why doesn't HubSpot catch duplicates with different emails?

HubSpot uses email as the unique key for contacts, so two records with different emails are two contacts as far as imports and forms are concerned. On plans that include it, the Manage duplicates tool suggests some pairs by name and other properties, but the contact list can be long and noisy, so check each pair before merging.

Is it safe to merge two contacts just because the names match?

No. Two different people can share a name. Look for a second link, such as the same phone number, the same company, or a personal email that clearly belongs to the same person, before you merge.

What happens to the second email when I merge?

HubSpot keeps the primary record's email and adds the other one as a secondary email on the merged contact, so mail to either address still finds the contact.

Can I undo a merge in HubSpot?

No. Merges are permanent, so export your contacts first and keep the file as a backup.
