A Hunter alternative for small-business websites

Hunter is very good at one job: give it a company domain and it tells you the people who work there and the likely format of their email addresses, then verifies them. If you sell to companies with a sales team and a LinkedIn page, it's hard to beat.

Local lead lists are a different problem. A plumber, a dental clinic or a family-run hotel usually has one inbox, a phone number, maybe a Facebook page and a contact form, and all of that is sitting on their own website. You don't need an email pattern, you need someone to open 500 sites and copy down what's there.

That's what Website Contact Finder does. It's an Apify actor. You paste in websites, company names or the dataset from a Google Maps scrape, and you get one row per business back.

Try Website Contact Finder on Apify

What you get for each business

  • Emails, best one first (a company-domain address is ranked above a free mailbox, and a team inbox is labelled as such)
  • Phone numbers in international format, with the country
  • Social profiles, address, the contact page and the contact form link
  • A status for every input, so you know which sites had nothing and which were unreachable

Sites that only load their content with JavaScript are opened in a real browser when needed. Obfuscated addresses like name [at] domain and Cloudflare-protected emails are decoded.

How the pricing works

You pay $0.004 for each website where contacts were found (it drops to $0.0025 on higher Apify plans). Sites with nothing on them, sites that are down, invalid inputs and repeats are free. There's no start fee and no monthly plan. Apify gives new accounts a free monthly credit, which is enough to test it on a real list.

Is it reliable?

I tested it on 1,186 real businesses and tech companies, twice: zero crashes, and every input came back with a row. If a run hits its time limit, the sites it didn't get to are listed as "Not checked" and you aren't charged for them.

When to stick with Hunter

If you need the email of a particular person, like the head of marketing at a 200-person company, use Hunter or a similar people-finder. Website Contact Finder can only return what the business has published, and about a third of the emails we knew about from other sources weren't on the business's site at all. It also has no browser extension or outreach tools. It's a data step, not a sales platform.

FAQ

Is Website Contact Finder a Hunter replacement?

For local and small-business lists, mostly yes. For finding named employees at larger companies, no. Hunter's pattern guessing and verification are built for that.

How much does it cost?

$4 per 1,000 websites where contacts are found on Apify's Free and Bronze plans, less on higher plans. Sites with no contacts, errors and duplicates cost nothing. There's no subscription.

Can I feed it a Google Maps scrape?

Yes. Give it the dataset ID from a Google Maps scraper run and it reads the website column for you.

Does it guess email addresses?

No. Every email it returns was published on the business's own site. It ranks them and checks that the domain can receive mail.

Do I need to code?

No. You can paste sites into the Apify form and download the results as CSV or Excel. There's also an API if you want to automate it.
